# Design Personality

Captured during a designer interview before stardust brand extraction. Downstream skills should treat this as a strong quality gate.

## Three-word personality

Considered. Exact. Dry.

## Aesthetic lane (chosen)

Editorial-typographic — but specifically the version that earns the label through tight typesetting and density of real content, not through serif-display-plus-generous-space pastiche. The risk in this lane is high: it is the saturated reflex for "tasteful engineer site" right now. Treat the lane as a starting point and apply one signature move (see below) that pulls the site out of the default.

## References (positive)

Worth pointing the brand and prototype phases at, not for surface mimicry, but for what they get right:

- **paulstamatiou.com** — long-form rigor, photography integrated as evidence, restrained chrome.
- **robinrendle.com** — typesetting-first, treats the page as a medium, comfortable with whitespace.
- **rauno.me** — one signature gesture (cursor / interaction touches) that feels load-bearing, not decorative.
- **frankchimero.com** — voice on the page, opinions stated, color used when it does work.

These are references, not templates. The goal is the level, not the look.

## Anti-references (specific things to refuse)

- Cream-or-off-white ground with a "Ship faster" hero, gradient blob behind it, three identical feature cards below. The current AI default. We must visibly reject this in the first viewport.
- Crypto neon, glassmorphism, gradient text, big hero metrics, side-stripe borders on cards.
- Awwwards-bait scroll choreography. No parallax. No autoplay video. No "wow on load."
- Resume-PDF-as-website (Helvetica + black-on-white + no point of view).
- Monospace-everywhere developer pastiche. Mono is allowed in code, in figures, in numerals; not as the body face.

## Rules I want followed

1. **Type carries the design.** Hierarchy through scale and weight, not through color or boxes. Cap measure 65–75ch.
2. **Color is punctuation.** Restrained or committed strategy with one earned accent. No five-role decorative palette.
3. **No em dashes in copy.** Use commas, colons, periods, parentheses. Also not `--`.
4. **Specificity over polish.** Name the systems. Use real numbers. Cite the constraints. Avoid "passionate about," "leveraging," "modern," "scalable" in the marketing sense.
5. **The site is the artifact.** Performance, semantic HTML, motion budget, accessibility — the implementation is part of the claim. If the copy says "considered," the source must agree.

## One rule I want broken

Editorial-typographic sites tend to be visually quiet end-to-end. I want **one signature gesture that is unmistakably Blake's** — load-bearing, not decorative, applied consistently across the site. It can be a typographic detail (a way numerals are set, a marginal annotation system, a specific way figures are captioned), an interaction touch (a cursor behavior, a scroll-position read-out), a structural quirk (a single-column footnote stream, a colophon page that's actually used), or a recurring motif tied to the niche (edge-network or commerce semantics rendered as visible page state).

The brand phase chooses one. The prototype phase makes it consistent. Without it, the site collapses into the saturated default.

## Niche anchor

Cloudflare-platform commerce and edge-AI. The site reads as the work of someone who builds at that specific intersection — not a generic "full-stack developer." Specificity in copy, project names, and any visual motif should reinforce this rather than fight it.

## Things that annoy me

- "Skills: React, TypeScript, Node.js" walls of logos.
- Buzzword resumes ("synergy-driven solutions architect").
- Hero sections that don't say what the thing is.
- Sites where motion exists because the framework supports it.
- Three-card "What I do" rows.
